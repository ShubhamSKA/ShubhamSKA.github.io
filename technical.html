Magnetically Actuated Haptic Displays

Versatile Electronic Systems Lab, Cornell University · Advisor: Prof. Amal El-Ghazaly · September 2026 – Present

The problem

A haptic display has to hold a shape long enough for a finger to read it. Most soft actuators hold their shape only while current keeps flowing, which sets the power budget by how long the display stays up rather than by how often it changes.

The approach

Electro-permanent magnets switch between two stable magnetic states with a current pulse and then hold that state with no standing current. Built into a soft actuator, that decouples holding a shape from powering the display.

My work is on the actuator's form factor — the geometries and material stacks that turn the magnetic switching into useful displacement at a scale a fingertip can resolve.

To expand: actuator geometries under test, materials, drive electronics, and displacement and force measurements.

EdemaFlex — Hand Edema Treatment Glove

Hybrid Body Lab, Cornell University · Advisor: Prof. Cindy Hsin-Liu Kao · August 2026 – Present

The problem

Compression therapy for hand edema generally means a garment fitted and applied by someone else. A patient with reduced hand function is exactly the person least able to put on a tight glove unaided.

The approach

EdemaFlex is a knit glove with shape memory alloy springs worked into the structure. The springs contract on heating, so the garment applies its own compression rather than relying on a tight fit, and can be put on slack.

My work

  • Manufacturing the knit gloves and integrating the shape memory alloy springs.
  • Updating the interface that generates the design files, so a glove's pattern can be produced for a given hand and treatment profile rather than hand-authored each time.

To expand: knit structure and spring routing, actuation and thermal control, pressure measurements, and the design-file generation pipeline.

FiberCircuits — System-on-Fiber

Responsive Environments, MIT Media Lab · Advisor: Prof. Joseph A. Paradiso · May 2026 – Present

The problem

Electronics woven into a textile stop reading as textile once the fiber is thick enough to feel. Width is the constraint that decides whether a sensing fiber can be worn all night without being noticed — which is the whole point for sleep applications.

The approach

I am building the next version of the FiberCircuits topology, taking the fiber from 1.4 mm down to under 1 mm while keeping it a complete system rather than a passive thread: components and interconnect topologies that support sleep detection and electrostimulation.

To expand: the topology itself, component selection and packaging at sub-millimetre width, the fabrication process, and what the fiber measures.

Machine Learning on Limited Materials Datasets

Nanomaterials Lab, Universidad Autónoma del Estado de Morelos · Advisor: Prof. Vivechana Agarwal · May 2026 – August 2026

The problem

Materials science datasets are small, expensive to collect, and sampled at whatever times the experiment allowed. Most models assume neither of those things: they want many samples on a regular grid. Applying standard methods to this data either overfits or forces the lab to collect data it cannot afford.

The approach

A time-agnostic model — one whose predictions do not depend on the measurements arriving at fixed intervals — trained for the small-sample regime.

Results

Contaminant classification 97% accuracy
Concentration prediction 0.061 mean absolute error
Status Manuscript in progress — Kumar Agarwal, Avalos, et al.

To expand: dataset size and provenance, model architecture, how time-agnosticism is achieved, and the baselines compared against.

Inverse Design of Photonic Devices

QuMETA NanoML Group, Purdue University · Advisors: Prof. Alexander V. Kildishev, Prof. Alexandra Boltasseva, Prof. Vladimir Shalaev · March 2025 – Present

The problem

Inverse design searches a space of candidate structures for one that meets an optical target. Scoring a candidate normally means running an electromagnetic solver, and that cost is what sets how much of the space can be explored. The field also lacks shared datasets, so results are hard to compare between groups.

What I built

  • A curated and validated metagrating dataset of 10,000+ device structures, intended for open source release so other groups can benchmark against the same data.
  • A VGGNet-based network that predicts device efficiency directly, removing the solver from the inner loop.
  • Performance benchmarks for a new inverse-design ML framework, by training four distinct baseline models across two custom datasets.

Results

Efficiency evaluation Over 100× faster than the solver, with minimal loss in accuracy
Dataset 10,000+ metagrating structures, curated and validated
Status Manuscript in progress — Kumar Agarwal, Iyer, et al.

To expand: dataset generation and validation procedure, network architecture and training setup, the four baselines, and accuracy against solver ground truth.

Wireless Battery State of Charge Estimation

Wireless Sensing Circuits and Systems Lab, Purdue University · Advisor: Prof. Byunghoo Jung · April 2023 – August 2025

The problem

State of charge cannot be measured directly. Online estimators infer it from terminal behaviour, which drifts while a cell relaxes after load — so a reading taken shortly after use is the least trustworthy one, and that is exactly when it is usually wanted.

What I built

  • A method for wireless online LiPo charge detection, measured non-invasively rather than through the terminals.
  • An STM32-based frequency counter covering a 30 MHz range at ±3 Hz error, with a Python GUI for real-time sensor data visualisation and logging.
  • Acquisition pipelines for multi-instrument battery datasets — filtering, noise removal, and synchronisation — to extract reliable parameters and validate sensor and device performance.

Results

Relaxation-induced error Under ±1%, superior to existing state-of-the-art online sensing methods
Frequency counter 30 MHz range, ±3 Hz error

To expand: the sensing mechanism, board design, the characterisation setup shown on the main site, and the comparison against other online estimators.

High Voltage Power Supply from Recycled Parts

Nanomaterials Lab, Universidad Autónoma del Estado de Morelos · Advisor: Prof. Vivechana Agarwal · July – September 2021

The problem

Electrospinning needs a high-voltage supply and a dip-coating stage. Commercial equipment for both is expensive enough to be the limiting factor on whether a lab can run the process at all.

What I built

A high voltage power supply and a dip coating device, both assembled from recycled electronic spare parts. The supply drives an electrospinning device that is still in use at the lab.

Output

  • Kumar Agarwal, S. S., Mendoza Enríquez, B. U., & Hernández Rivera, D. (2022). Device Fabrication from Recycled Electronic Spare Parts: Dip Coating Device and High Voltage Power Supply Adapted for Electrospinning Device. Journal of Physics: Conference Series, 2261, 012001. DOI
  • Best Presenter, 2022 International Conference on Information and Electronics Engineering.

To expand: supply topology, output voltage and regulation, which parts were recovered and from what, and the electrospinning results it enabled.

StereoBoy — Modular Expandable MP3 Player

Senior Design, Purdue University · Best Electrical and Computer Engineering Senior Design Project, 2026 Purdue Spark Challenge

Hear. There. Everywhere. StereoBoy is a pocket-sized, battery-powered lossless audio player built around a cartridge system. It is the same size as a GameBoy Pocket, runs for about five hours on two AAA alkaline cells, and is designed to be opened up and changed — the cartridge interface carries storage, firmware and expansion hardware, so a user can extend the device rather than replace it.

Quick specs

MCU RP2350B dual-core ARM Cortex-M33F @ 150 MHz
Audio 24-bit 192 kHz lossless stereo codec, DAC and headphone amp; FLAC support
Display 1.54" 240×240 TFT LCD, 16-bit colour, plus twin high-brightness LED VU meters
Storage micro-SD for music, QSPI flash for firmware, 8 KB FRAM for fast nonvolatile state
Power 2× AAA alkaline, ~5 hour battery life; USB-C with priority mux
Output 3.5 mm TRRS and mono speaker
Features Automatic metadata management, real-time 6-band equaliser, real-time reactive audio visualisers

Audio pipeline

Music is read from the micro-SD card by the RP2350, which handles playback control and transfers the stream to the audio codec while rendering visualisers in parallel. The codec decodes and passes audio to the DAC and amplifier stage, which applies equalisation and volume control before amplification.

Measured on a 1 kHz sine wave played back as a 320 kbps 44.1 kHz MP3, with the rail characterised at 2.0 V battery input:

3.3 V rail at playback 3.3347 V average, 42 mV peak-to-peak
Regulation 0.91% accuracy, ±0.38% precision

Power supply

A "double-decker" supply runs the entire system from two AAA cells without compromising noise or efficiency: a 5 V boost from the battery, feeding 3.3 V and 1.8 V bucks, with a priority mux that hands over to USB-C when it is connected.

StereoMag cartridge interface

StereoMag is the physical storage medium for music, software and additional hardware. It is designed to let users share music and custom software with each other, and to let power users augment the device to fit their needs.

Flash memory micro-SD music storage, QSPI firmware storage, optional 16 MB PSRAM expansion
HSTX 8-bit DDR GPIO, 2.4 Gbps total throughput, reconfigurable as SPI / I2C / UART / I2S / HDMI
Misc. I/O SPI, I2C, I2S
Power 5.0 V and 3.3 V rails

Cartridges

  • RadioMag — FM receiver with an integrated PCB antenna and headset antenna, frequency seeking and presets. Retains the StereoBoy firmware.
  • SensorMag — temperature, humidity and pressure sensing, real-time clock, 6-DoF IMU and a visible light colour sensor, driving sensor-based playlist generation and shake-to-shuffle.
  • PhotoMag — CVBS video camera with an ADV7180 video decoder ASIC, interfaced over GPIO, capturing images to the micro-SD card.
  • Afterburner — ±10 V split-rail supply, 32-bit 384 kHz lossless DAC and amplifier, line-level stereo out, precision RTC, 8 KB FRAM and 16 MB PSRAM.
  • StereoMag Developer Kit — exposed pin header for prototyping, plus 16 MB PSRAM and 8 KB FRAM.

Firmware

The StereoBoy Modular Machine Firmware splits the two cores by concern. Core 0 handles user I/O, filesystem I/O and device I/O, and runs jukebox.c. Core 1 runs the visualisers, user interface and 2D graphics rendering. They meet at 520 KB of shared SRAM plus a QSPI XIP cache holding the framebuffer, audio and metadata, status registers and ADC samples.

Built on the Pico SDK 2.2.0 and ARM CMSIS over the ARMv8-M instruction set, with FatFs for the filesystem and a device driver layer beneath sb_util.c and filehelper.c.
