Inverse Design of Photonic Devices

QuMETA NanoML Group, Purdue University · Advisors: Prof. Alexander V. Kildishev, Prof. Alexandra Boltasseva, Prof. Vladimir Shalaev · March 2025 – Present

The problem

Inverse design searches a space of candidate structures for one that meets an optical target. Scoring a candidate normally means running an electromagnetic solver, and that cost is what sets how much of the space can be explored. The field also lacks shared datasets, so results are hard to compare between groups.

What I built

  • A curated and validated diffractive metagrating dataset of 10,000+ device structures, intended for open source release so other groups can benchmark against the same data.
  • Two VGGNet-based networks that attempt to predict device efficiency directly, removing the solver from the inner loop. One of the models is trained to predict efficiencies for the diffractive metagrating cells, while the other is for a pre-existing thermophotovoltaic dataset.
  • I recreated four models, namely, an Adversarial Auto-Encoder, a Wasserstein Generative Adversarial Network, and their conditional counterparts. I created two versions of each, one for each dataset, and trained them all.
  • An extensive comparison of models for inverse design in photonics, soon to be published.

Results

Efficiency evaluation Over 100× faster than the solver, with minimal loss in accuracy
Dataset 10,000+ metagrating structures, curated and validated
Status Manuscript in progress: Kumar Agarwal, Iyer, et al.
