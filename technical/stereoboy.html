StereoBoy: Modular Expandable MP3 Player

Senior Design, Purdue University · Best Electrical and Computer Engineering Senior Design Project, 2026 Purdue Spark Challenge · 2026

Hear. There. Everywhere. StereoBoy is a pocket-sized, battery-powered lossless audio player built around a cartridge system. It is the same size as a GameBoy Pocket, runs for about five hours on two AAA alkaline cells, and is designed to be opened up and changed: the cartridge interface carries storage, firmware and expansion hardware, so a user can extend the device rather than replace it.

Quick specs

MCU RP2350B dual-core ARM Cortex-M33F @ 150 MHz
Audio 24-bit 192 kHz lossless stereo codec, DAC and headphone amp; FLAC support
Display 1.54" 240×240 TFT LCD, 16-bit colour, plus twin high-brightness LED VU meters
Storage micro-SD for music, QSPI flash for firmware, 8 KB FRAM for fast nonvolatile state
Power 2× AAA alkaline, ~5 hour battery life; USB-C with priority mux
Output 3.5 mm TRRS and mono speaker
Features Automatic metadata management, real-time 6-band equaliser, real-time reactive audio visualisers

Audio pipeline

Music is read from the micro-SD card by the RP2350, which handles playback control and transfers the stream to the audio codec while rendering visualisers in parallel. The codec decodes and passes audio to the DAC and amplifier stage, which applies equalisation and volume control before amplification.

Measured on a 1 kHz sine wave played back as a 320 kbps 44.1 kHz MP3, with the rail characterised at 2.0 V battery input:

3.3 V rail at playback 3.3347 V average, 42 mV peak-to-peak
Regulation 0.91% accuracy, ±0.38% precision

Power supply

A "double-decker" supply runs the entire system from two AAA cells without compromising noise or efficiency: a 5 V boost from the battery, feeding 3.3 V and 1.8 V bucks, with a priority mux that hands over to USB-C when it is connected.

StereoMag cartridge interface

StereoMag is the physical storage medium for music, software and additional hardware. It is designed to let users share music and custom software with each other, and to let power users augment the device to fit their needs.

Flash memory micro-SD music storage, QSPI firmware storage, optional 16 MB PSRAM expansion
HSTX 8-bit DDR GPIO, 2.4 Gbps total throughput, reconfigurable as SPI / I2C / UART / I2S / HDMI
Misc. I/O SPI, I2C, I2S
Power 5.0 V and 3.3 V rails

Cartridges

  • RadioMag: FM receiver with an integrated PCB antenna and headset antenna, frequency seeking and presets. Retains the StereoBoy firmware.
  • SensorMag: temperature, humidity and pressure sensing, real-time clock, 6-DoF IMU and a visible light colour sensor, driving sensor-based playlist generation and shake-to-shuffle.
  • PhotoMag: CVBS video camera with an ADV7180 video decoder ASIC, interfaced over GPIO, capturing images to the micro-SD card.
  • Afterburner: ±10 V split-rail supply, 32-bit 384 kHz lossless DAC and amplifier, line-level stereo out, precision RTC, 8 KB FRAM and 16 MB PSRAM.
  • StereoMag Developer Kit: exposed pin header for prototyping, plus 16 MB PSRAM and 8 KB FRAM.

Firmware

The StereoBoy Modular Machine Firmware splits the two cores by concern. Core 0 handles user I/O, filesystem I/O and device I/O, and runs jukebox.c. Core 1 runs the visualisers, user interface and 2D graphics rendering. They meet at 520 KB of shared SRAM plus a QSPI XIP cache holding the framebuffer, audio and metadata, status registers and ADC samples.

Built on the Pico SDK 2.2.0 and ARM CMSIS over the ARMv8-M instruction set, with FatFs for the filesystem and a device driver layer beneath sb_util.c and filehelper.c.
