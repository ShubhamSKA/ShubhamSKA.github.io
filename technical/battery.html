Wireless Battery State of Charge Estimation

Wireless Sensing Circuits and Systems Lab, Purdue University · Advisor: Prof. Byunghoo Jung · April 2023 – August 2025

The problem

State of charge cannot be measured directly. Online estimators infer it from terminal behaviour, which drifts while a cell relaxes after load. Thus readings taken shortly after use are not trustworthy.

What I built

  • I helped design and test a method for wireless online LiPo state-of-charge detection, measured non-invasively rather than through the terminals. The circuit itself was not designed by me, but a large majority of it's measurement digitization and interpretation fell under my purview.
  • The output of the circuit is a sinusoid, and it's amplitude and frequency change based on the state of charge. So, I designed an STM32-based frequency counter covering a 30 MHz range at ±3 Hz error.
  • I also developed a Python GUI for real-time sensor data visualisation and logging.
  • Acquisition pipelines for multi-instrument battery datasets (filtering, noise removal, and synchronisation) to extract reliable parameters and validate sensor and device performance.
  • I also developed all the denoising and data analysis algorithms.
  • I also assembled over 20 PCBs across several vesrsions of the cricuit, each with over 100 SMD components.

Results

Relaxation-induced error Under ±1%, superior to existing state-of-the-art online sensing methods
Frequency counter 30 MHz range, ±3 Hz error
