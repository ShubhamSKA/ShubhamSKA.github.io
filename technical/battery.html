Wireless Battery State of Charge Estimation

Wireless Sensing Circuits and Systems Lab, Purdue University · Advisor: Prof. Byunghoo Jung · April 2023 – August 2025

The problem

State of charge cannot be measured directly. Online estimators infer it from terminal behaviour, which drifts while a cell relaxes after load, so a reading taken shortly after use is the least trustworthy one. That is usually exactly when you want it.

What I built

I helped design and test a method for detecting LiPo state of charge wirelessly, measuring the cell non-invasively rather than through its terminals. The circuit itself was not mine, but most of the measurement digitisation and interpretation was.

That circuit outputs a sinusoid whose amplitude and frequency shift with state of charge, so reading it precisely enough is the whole problem. My side of it:

  • An STM32-based frequency counter covering a 30 MHz range at ±3 Hz error.
  • A Python GUI for visualising and logging the sensor data in real time.
  • The acquisition pipeline behind the multi-instrument datasets: filtering, denoising and synchronisation, plus the analysis that turns the raw captures into parameters we could trust.

I also assembled over 20 boards across several versions of the circuit, each carrying more than 100 SMD components.

Results

Relaxation-induced error Under ±1%, better than existing state-of-the-art online sensing methods
