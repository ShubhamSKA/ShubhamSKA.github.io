2-Player Snake Game

ECE 36200, Purdue University · 2024 · with Craig Eagleburger, Andrew Larkins and Brian Diaz · Source on GitHub

Each player gets a board of their own: a microcontroller, an LCD and four buttons. Both boards run the same firmware, and neither one is in charge. Each keeps its own copy of the game and draws it on its own screen, and the two stay in step by telling each other only what they have to, over four wires.

This game needs a keyboard, so the boards only run on a laptop or desktop.

Loading the firmware.

Slowed down so you can see it: at 115200 baud a byte spends 87 µs on the wire.

Playing

  • Press Power on. The two boards start a moment apart, the way two boards plugged in by hand would.
  • Board A takes W A S D and board B the arrow keys. You can also click the buttons under each screen.
  • Press a key on each side to start. Whichever board sees its button first is player 1, the blue snake from the top-left corner. The other is player 2, the green snake from the bottom-right.
  • A snake only turns: pressing the way it is already going, or the way it came from, does nothing. The game speeds up with every apple.
  • When a snake hits a wall, itself or the other snake, both screens turn the winner's colour, or split in two for a tie. To play again, reset both boards.

What the wires carry

Ready line A plain GPIO line that means ready. Each board sets its own line high as soon as it has started, then sleeps until the other's comes up, which it hears as a rising-edge interrupt.
'p' The first button pressed on either board sends 'p' over the UART. The board that sent it is player 1; the board that received it becomes player 2, and sends its own 'p' once its player presses a button.
Seed When player 2's 'p' arrives, player 1 reads its TIM6 counter and sends that as the random seed. Both boards call srand with it, so every apple lands in the same place on both screens.
Direction On every tick each board sends the direction its player last pressed (0 right, 1 up, 2 left, 3 down) and waits for the other's. Only then does it move both snakes, so the two copies of the game never drift apart.

How it runs

Nothing on this page is a rewrite of the game. The firmware is compiled, unchanged, from the project's own source, and runs on two emulated microcontrollers wired together like the real boards. Everything on the wires and on the screens comes from that firmware. As on the real chips, each board's clock runs very slightly fast or slow; without that, every game would get the same random seed.
