About Me

I build the physical sensing layer for interfaces that adapt to people, instead of asking people to adapt to them — the wearable hardware, the firmware that runs on it, and the signal processing that makes its measurements worth trusting.

In practice that has meant non-invasive battery state estimation at the Wireless Sensing Circuits and Systems Lab, machine learning for photonic inverse design with QuMETA, and an evaluation pipeline still in use at the Low Power Computer Vision Challenge. I hold a BS in Computer Engineering from Purdue University and am completing a Master of Engineering at Cornell University.

Work Experience

Research

Non-invasive battery state-of-charge estimation — Wireless Sensing Circuits and Systems Lab, Purdue University.

Problem. State of charge cannot be measured directly, and online estimators drift badly while a cell relaxes after load.
Built. The sensing chain end to end: acquisition firmware on the microcontroller, the boards it runs on, the measurement setup, and the data pipeline behind it.
Result. Under 0.1% estimation error with roughly 1% relaxation drift — about 5× better than state-of-the-art online estimators.
Artifact. Manuscript in preparation.

April 2023-Present

Benchmarking ML models for thermophotovoltaic inverse design — NanoML, QuMETA group, Purdue University.

Problem. Inverse-design work needs a dataset and a set of baselines other groups can actually build on, rather than one-off results.
Built. The benchmarking of a thermophotovoltaic inverse-design dataset, including training and evaluating an adversarial autoencoder against it.
Status. Ongoing; the benchmark is intended for reuse by later work.

March 2025-Present

Evaluation framework for the Low Power Computer Vision Challenge — Vertically Integrated Program, Purdue University.

Problem. An international challenge needs a submission and scoring pipeline that ranks 3D-reconstruction models fairly under a hard power budget.
Built. The submission pipeline and the evaluation model itself, plus an open-source reference solution entrants must beat to qualify.
Result. The framework was adopted the year after I left and used to judge 59 teams across 516 submitted solutions.
Artifact. lpcv.ai — the challenge runs on it.
